There are events you attend for a certificate, and then there are events that genuinely change the way you think about your field. VulnCon was the latter for me.

For those who haven't heard of it — VulnCon is India's fastest-growing technical cybersecurity conference, laser-focused on core research, offensive security, and practical innovation. It's not a generic tech expo; it's a room full of people who actually care about the deep, sometimes uncomfortable, mechanics of how systems break — and how to fix them. The conference brings together security researchers, red teamers, DFIR practitioners, CISOs, and students all under one roof, and the energy is unlike anything I've experienced at a standard corporate conference.

## Stepping Into the Arena

Walking in on Day 1, the atmosphere hit immediately. Booths from sponsors like Altered Security and CloudSEK, a live CTF buzzing in the background, villages dedicated to AI security hands-on labs — it was dense and deliberate. Every corner had something substantive going on.

The talks were genuinely technical. Not "here's a framework slide deck" technical — I mean researchers walking through real attack chains, real CVEs, real tooling. Sessions covered everything from weaponizing DNS TXT records and Linux kernel malware to AI agent exploitation and multi-cloud threat hunting. The bar was refreshingly high.

## The Moment I Met Ansh Bhavnani

If you're into Indian cybersecurity content, you probably already know Ansh Bhavnani — the creator who's been doing some of the most accessible yet genuinely insightful cybersecurity content on YouTube for an Indian audience. His channel has been a go-to for people trying to break into the field without the gatekeeping that usually comes with it.

Meeting him in person at VulnCon was one of those moments where the internet becomes real. He was exactly as you'd expect — approachable, curious, and clearly someone who uses conferences like this to learn, not just to network. We ended up talking about the growing cybersecurity creator space in India, how the community is maturing, and the gap between academic certifications and actual hands-on skill.

What struck me most was his perspective on community — that India has all the talent it needs, it just needs more spaces like VulnCon to channel it. Hard to disagree when you're standing in the middle of exactly that.

## What I Took Away

VulnCon isn't trying to be DEF CON or Black Hat — and that's the point. It's building something rooted in the Indian security landscape, with local context and local voices front and center, while still pulling in international speakers and global-caliber research.

Meeting someone like Ansh in that space made it feel even more significant. The content creators, the researchers, the red teamers, the students — they're all part of the same ecosystem, and events like this are where that ecosystem becomes a community.

If you haven't been to VulnCon yet, put it on your list. The next edition is already shaping up to be even bigger. And if you happen to run into Ansh there — stop and say hi. He's genuinely one of the good ones.
