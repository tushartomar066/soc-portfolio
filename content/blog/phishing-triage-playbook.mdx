Phishing is the highest-volume alert type in most SOCs, and most of the triage
work is mechanical. Here's how I automated it.

## The manual workflow (before)

A reported phish used to take ~45 minutes:

1. Extract headers and check SPF/DKIM/DMARC
2. Pull out URLs and attachments
3. Detonate each URL in a sandbox
4. Enrich IOCs against VirusTotal and the internal blocklist
5. Decide: benign, spam, or malicious
6. If malicious — purge from all mailboxes

## The automated playbook

Steps 1–4 are pure enrichment, perfect for SOAR. The playbook fires on a new
"Reported Phishing" incident and runs enrichment in parallel.

```python
# Pseudocode for the enrichment branch
def enrich(email):
    auth = check_email_auth(email.headers)      # SPF / DKIM / DMARC
    urls = extract_urls(email.body)
    verdicts = [detonate(u) for u in urls]       # sandbox each URL
    iocs = virustotal_lookup(email.attachments)
    return score(auth, verdicts, iocs)
```

## Scoring and auto-closure

I assign a weighted score. Anything below the benign threshold is auto-closed
with a note; anything above the malicious threshold triggers mailbox purge and
pages the on-call analyst.

> **Key lesson:** never let automation *delete* on its own without a human gate
> early on. Build trust in the scoring first, then expand auto-actions.

## Results

After two months of tuning:

- **MTTR:** 45m → 8m
- **Analyst touches per phish:** ~6 → ~1
- **Auto-closed (benign):** 60% of reports

The analysts got their time back for the investigations that actually need a
human brain.
